We watched Osman Uncle billow out a ghost from his mouth.

Two perfect smoke rings, first a big ring and then a smaller ring which passes through the big one easily. His once white kurta now stained yellow, smells like smoke and burning paper.

He is an old man hired by my grandparents to guard our house in the gullies of Sanathnagar. A ‘*chowkidar*’- he took his job seriously- when night came, he wore his navy-blue uniform with black collars and a dark navy cap. With his shirt tucked in neatly, the look is completed with a belt and a flashlight hanging from one loop, along with a long sturdy stick. He looked more like a mall guard than a chowkidar of an old house occasionally invaded by rats and lowly thieves we’d lose rusted pipes and empty gas cylinders to. He would sit on a pale pink plastic chair just behind the gates, and guard vigilantly. When morning came, he would transform into any other *musalman* in our *mohalla*, wearing a namaz cap and a once white kurta now stained yellow.

Back then, school would end by noon and everyday felt like summer vacation, and summer vacation felt like grandma’s house- fly-specked walls, dusty corridors and empty rooms for playing hide and seek, a pressure cooker going off in the kitchen with the smell of lamb kibbeh wafting up to the first floor, the clambering thuds of our small feet up and down the stairs during an energetic game of ‘*dippa*’, and Osman uncle’s long-winded chowkidar stories and cigarette tricks.

He smoked furtively when the adults were around, and proudly like a circus performer displaying his craft when it was just us. We would gather around him on his plastic chair and watch diligently like little circus clowns, spongy brains ready to learn.

Osman Uncle would take a deep breath like he was going underwater forever, the tip of the blunt burning brightly like hot coals, then he would blow out huge swirls of tantalizing smoke trails which curved and undulated in the sunlight. Sometimes he would suck the tobacco in through his mouth and then block one nostril with a forefinger and a whole freight train would emerge through the other nostril. We would watch in awe, jaws dropped, begging him to teach us this sorcery.

How was it that there was a secret passage connecting the mouth and the nose?

Horrifying. Were we full of holes inside?
